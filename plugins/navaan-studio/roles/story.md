Think first, then write:
1. emotion: one sentence naming what the song feels like and the relationship at its heart (who is longing for whom, what is unsaid, how it turns). Read ALL the lyrics before you decide. The frames must follow the song from its first line to its last, in order, so that someone watching with the sound on sees each part of the story when they hear it.
2. character: the people the camera follows, as ONE description an image model can repeat word for word and a second image can match: for each person give age range, build, face and hair (and facial hair), skin tone, the clothes, and one small signature detail that stays in every shot (a silver ring, a rust coloured scarf, a watch). The main person is the singer's presence in the film and MUST match the singer (see the voice note above). Add a second person ONLY if the lyrics clearly address or describe one (a beloved, someone who left, a friend, a parent), and then match who the lyrics name. Do not invent a romantic partner for a song that is not about one: a song about the self, the city, memory, work, hope or resilience has one person alone, or no person. Clothes stay the same through the film unless a lyric gives a reason (a rain jacket over the same sweater).
3. One scene per numbered slot, in order. Each scene says what the people are doing and feeling, then where they are. The place is only the setting for the feeling.

What makes a scene good:
- Scene 1 is the REFERENCE picture that every later picture is matched to, and only the people who appear in it will look the same later. So scene 1 must show EVERY main person from your character description (the main person and the beloved, if there is one) in one medium or medium wide shot, together in the frame (side by side, across a table, one in the foreground and one further away, one in a window or doorway), each clearly visible: three quarter or side view, face lit well enough to read, a natural, thoughtful expression. It still carries the first lines of the song.
- Faces are welcome. In at least half of the frames a face is visible and readable: a quiet look out of a window, a half smile that fades, eyes lowered, a reflection in glass. Feeling shows in the face and the posture, usually not in tears. Use silhouettes, backs, reflections and hands for the rest, and keep a sharp extreme close up of a face rare.
- A moment between people, or a person alone feeling someone's absence: two figures sharing an umbrella in rain, one waiting under a streetlight, a hand resting on a railing where another hand was, someone reading a letter by a window, two silhouettes on a rooftop at dusk, walking apart on a long road, a lit window across the street, a train pulling away.
- The emotion of each lyric is the scene. Do not draw the lyric's objects literally. A line about an unwritten letter is a person sitting with a blank page and a faraway look, not a post box.
- At most 3 of the <<n>> frames may show only objects or empty places (a letter, a cup, a window). Every other frame has at least one person in it.
- No markets, shop shutters, bookshelves or stock still lifes unless a lyric clearly asks for them.
- Follow the mood arc of the song: begin quiet and distant, build warmth and closeness, break at the turn, end resolved or aching. Vary wide, medium and close frames, and vary place, time of day and weather while the look the art director fixed stays the same. Choose places that suit the story and the culture of the song (a rain wet lane with a lit tea stall, a station platform, a window with city lights, a rooftop, a bus stop, a river promenade, a stairwell, a quiet cafe, a balcony), busy or empty as the feeling needs.
- Leave the subject and some empty space placed so a slow camera push, pull or pan has somewhere to go, and so lyrics can sit over a calm part of the frame.

For each scene also give a motion hint: the one or two things that could softly move in a loop without breaking the picture (rain on glass, a curtain, a dupatta or hair in the wind, a flame, steam, ripples, falling leaves, lights as bokeh, smoke). Name what moves and where. If nothing should move, write "none".

Prompt style: each scene is two or three sentences of concrete visual description for an image model (people, what they do, place, light, lens feel, one telling detail). Plain English even when the lyrics are not English. Do not repeat the look inside the scenes. Repeat the character description word for word, or a clear short form of it with the same signature detail, in the scenes where those people appear.
