Think first, then write:
1. emotion: one sentence naming what the song feels like and the relationship at its heart (who is longing for whom, what is unsaid, how it turns). Read ALL the lyrics before you decide. The frames must follow the song from its first line to its last, in order, so that someone watching with the sound on sees each part of the story when they hear it.
2. character: the main person the camera follows, in one sentence the image model can repeat word for word: age range, build, clothes, one memorable colour or object. For a love or longing song add the other person too (the beloved, the one who left, the one waiting) as a second line. People are the heart of the film. Show them from behind, from the side, in silhouette, in a reflection, in a doorway, at a window, hands almost touching, two figures close or far apart. Faces stay soft or turned away, never a sharp close up.
3. One scene per numbered slot, in order. Each scene says what the people are doing and feeling, then where they are. The place is only the setting for the feeling.

What makes a scene good:
- A moment between people, or a person alone feeling someone's absence: two figures sharing an umbrella in rain, one waiting under a streetlight, a hand resting on a railing where another hand was, someone reading a letter by a window at dusk, two silhouettes on a rooftop at golden hour, walking apart on a long road, a lit window across the street, a train pulling away.
- The emotion of each lyric is the scene. Do not draw the lyric's objects literally. A line about an unwritten letter is a person sitting with a blank page and a faraway look, not a post box.
- At most 3 of the <<n>> frames may show only objects or empty places (a letter, a cup, a window). Every other frame has at least one person in it.
- No markets, shop shutters, bookshelves or stock still lifes unless a lyric clearly asks for them.
- Variety in the mood arc: begin quiet and distant, build warmth and closeness, break at the turn, end resolved or aching, as the song does. Vary wide, medium and close detail frames, and vary time of day and weather while the palette stays consistent.
- Use places the singer's culture and the lyrics suggest: a rooftop, a balcony, a lane in rain, a river ghat at dusk, a courtyard, a train platform, a window in monsoon. Invent more.
- Leave the subject and some empty space placed so a slow camera push, pull or pan has somewhere to go.

For each scene also give a motion hint: the one or two things that could softly move in a loop without breaking the picture (rain on glass, a curtain, a dupatta or hair in the wind, a flame, steam, ripples, falling leaves, lights as bokeh, smoke). Name what moves and where. If nothing should move, write "none".

Prompt style: each scene is two or three sentences of concrete visual description for an image model (people, what they do, place, light, lens feel, one telling detail). Plain English even when the lyrics are not English. Do not repeat the look inside the scenes. Repeat the character lines word for word in the scenes where those people appear.
